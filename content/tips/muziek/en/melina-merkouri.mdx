## Melina Merkouri – The Soul of Greece

_Melina Merkouri (Μέλινα Μερκούρη)_ was not just a singer or actress — she **embodied** modern 
Greece. With her raspy voice, fiery personality and deep love for her country, she became the face 
of a generation yearning for freedom, music and pride.

### From the Theater to Hollywood

Melina was born in 1920 in **Athens**, into a family of politicians and artists. She began her 
career as a **stage actress**, but broke through internationally with the film **_Never on 
Sunday_ (1960)** by **Jules Dassin**, who later became her husband. The song 
_"Τα Παιδιά του Πειραιά" (Ta Pedia Tou Pirea)_ — sung by Melina herself — won an **Oscar** for Best 
Song and became a worldwide symbol of Greek optimism and joie de vivre.

> "As long as I can sing about Piraeus, Greece remains free," Melina later said in an interview.

### Voice of Resistance 🇬🇷

When the military dictatorship (the _Junta_) came to power in Greece in 1967, Melina had her 
**passport confiscated** and her **music banned**. In exile in Paris, she kept performing and spoke 
openly about freedom and democracy. Her performances became political manifestos — songs full of 
longing and hope.

<InfoBox title="Did you know?">
When the Junta stripped her of her citizenship, she said: <strong>"I was born a Greek, I will die a Greek. And you are only temporary."</strong>
</InfoBox>

### Return and Politics

After the fall of the dictatorship in 1974, Melina returned to Athens, where she joined the 
**Panhellenic Socialist Movement (PASOK)**. She later became **Minister of Culture** and launched 
the idea of the **European Capital of Culture** — a project that still exists today.

Melina believed culture was the soul of Europe:

> "A country without culture is like a body without a heart."

### The Fight for the Parthenon Marbles 🏛️

One of the most passionate causes of Melina's life was the **return of the Parthenon Marbles** — 
the famous sculptures that have been kept in the **British Museum in London** since the 19th 
century.

As Minister of Culture in the 1980s, Melina led a worldwide campaign to bring the marble sculptures 
back to Athens, where they once formed part of the **Parthenon on the Acropolis**.

> "There are no such things as the Elgin Marbles," Melina said with her characteristic fire. "They 
are the Parthenon Marbles — and they belong in Greece."

Melina spoke at conferences, wrote open letters, and managed to draw international attention to the 
Greek cause — something that lives on to this day. Her dedication inspired the creation of the 
**Acropolis Museum** in Athens, where an empty space now symbolically awaits the missing sculptures.

<InfoBox title="Melina's Legacy">
Today, Greek politicians, artists and citizens still often refer to Melina Merkouri's campaign as a 
symbol of cultural pride and justice. The call to bring the sculptures back to Greece rings louder 
than ever.
</InfoBox>

### Music & Legacy 🎶

Although Melina is best known for her films, music remained a constant thread throughout her life. 
Her voice — raw, emotional and unforgettable — can still be heard today in cafés in Athens and on 
radio stations in Crete.

### Visit Her World

Anyone visiting Athens can still find traces of Melina's life:

- The **Melina Merkouri Cultural Center** in **Thisio**
- The **Plaka neighborhood**, where she loved to walk
- The **port of Piraeus**, where _Ta Pedia Tou Pirea_ is set

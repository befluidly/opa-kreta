_Nana Mouskouri (Νάνα Μούσχουρη)_ is one of the most famous Greek singers of all time. She was born 
in **Chania**, on the island of **Crete**, and grew up surrounded by the sounds of traditional 
Greek music. From a young age, she sang in cafés and on local radio stations, where her unique 
voice immediately stood out — clear, soft and powerful all at once.

> "Her voice was like a crystal-clear Cretan stream making its way through the mountains," a 
French journalist once wrote.
> And that's exactly how she was heard: pure, emotional and recognizable from the very first note.

## Her Career

In the 1960s, Nana moved to **Paris**, where she worked with the famous composer **Michel 
Legrand**. That's where her international career began, with timeless songs like 
_"White Rose of Athens"_, _"Only Love"_ and _"Plaisir d'Amour"_.

She sang in more than **fifteen languages**, released more than **300 albums**, and sold millions 
of records worldwide. What made her so special? She always remained _Greek_ — even while standing 
on the biggest stages in the world.

<InfoBox title="Did you know?">
Nana Mouskouri was also a **Greek member of parliament** and a **UNICEF ambassador**. She dedicated 
years to helping children in need while continuing to perform — well into her seventies!
</InfoBox>

## Musical Style and Influence

Nana combined **Greek melodies** with **jazz**, **chanson**, **classical music** and **folk**. Her 
voice had no big vibrato, no shouting, but rather a calm clarity that touched millions of people. 
She worked with artists such as **Harry Belafonte**, **Quincy Jones** and **Julio Iglesias** — but 
always preferred singing in her mother tongue.

In Greece, she's seen as a **cultural symbol**: a woman who proudly carries her roots, while also 
building a bridge between East and West.

## Personal Connection to Crete 🇬🇷

Although she spent most of her life in France, Nana remained deeply connected to her birthplace, 
**Chania**. She returned there regularly — for concerts, family visits, or simply to walk through 
the old Venetian harbor.

In local cafés, her voice can still be heard on the radio, alongside classics by Melina Merkouri 
and Mikis Theodorakis. Sometimes you'll hear Nana's own versions of songs that evoke the old 
Greece — melodies reminiscent of films like _Never on Sunday_, in which **Melina Merkouri** sang 
the iconic _"Ta Pedia Tou Pirea"_.

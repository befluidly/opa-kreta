\*There are names inseparably linked to the soul of Greece, and **Mikis Theodorakis** is one of 
them. His music is more than melody — it's identity, struggle and emotion poured into notes. 
Anyone who's ever heard the rhythm of **"Zorba's Dance"** has, without realizing it, felt a piece of 
Greece: the vastness of the sea, the sun on your skin, the balance between melancholy and love of 
life.\*

## The Sound of a Generation

Mikis Theodorakis was born in 1925 on the island, but his music would soon transcend the borders 
of the Aegean Sea. He grew up in a time of great upheaval: war, occupation, civil conflict. This 
turbulent context shaped his musical and political consciousness. His compositions speak not only 
to the ear, but to the conscience — they breathe **resistance, hope and the strength of the 
people**.

Theodorakis wrote classical works, film scores and folk songs, but his style always remained 
recognizable: a fusion of **Greek folk sounds with symphonic depth**. He gave the music of the 
people a new dignity, without losing its simplicity.

## Zorba and the World

In 1964, Theodorakis conquered the world with his score for the film _Zorba the Greek_, based on 
the novel by **Nikos Kazantzakis**. The iconic theme, with its slowly building rhythm, grew into a 
**cultural symbol**. It became more than a film melody — it became an expression of the Greek 
spirit itself: the ability to dance, no matter what.

The **sirtaki**, the dance that became inseparable from this music, is by now just as well-known 
as the Acropolis. The Zorba melody was played, sung or whistled all over the world, from beachside 
taverns to concert halls.

## Voice of Resistance

Besides being a composer, Theodorakis was a man of conviction. During the **colonels' junta** 
(1967–1974), his music was banned, just like that of 
[Melina Merkouri](/en/tips/muziek/melina-merkouri), and he himself was imprisoned and exiled. Yet he 
kept writing — sometimes in secret — and his songs became a **sound of freedom** for the Greeks. 
His collaboration with poets such as **Yannis Ritsos** and **Odysseas Elytis** brought poetry and 
politics together in a way rarely matched since.

## Legacy and Eternity

Mikis Theodorakis passed away in 2021, but his music lives on in living rooms, theaters and town 
squares. On **Crete** — where Zorba's dance still springs up spontaneously at sunset — his melody 
sounds as though it had always been there.

His work teaches that music is not just an art form, but also a way of existing: with strength, 
passion and dignity. Anyone who listens to Theodorakis hears **Greece itself** — in all its light 
and fighting spirit.

Mikis Theodorakis is buried on Crete in Galatas, west of Chania, next to his parents and his 
brother Giannis.

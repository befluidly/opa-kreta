## Ingredients

For 4 people:

- 500 g Greek or other spicy pork sausage (e.g. loukaniko, or a merguez-style sausage) – I often use chorizo and farmer's sausage as a substitute for loukaniko
- 3 bell peppers (green, red and yellow for color), cut into rough pieces (3x3 cm)
- 1 large onion, cut into rough pieces
- 2 cloves garlic, finely sliced
- 2 to 3 tbsp olive oil
- 1 to 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 400 g peeled tomatoes (canned or fresh, cut into pieces) – I usually use fresh tomatoes
- 60 ml ouzo
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- Salt and pepper, to taste
- Optionally, a pinch of chili or a few drops of Tabasco

## Preparation

1. **Fry the sausage**
Slice the sausages into rounds. Heat some olive oil in a wide pan and
fry the pieces of sausage over medium heat until golden brown. Remove them from the pan and set aside.

2. **Sauté the vegetables**
In the same pan, sauté the onion and garlic until translucent. Add the
peppers and let them cook for about 10 minutes, until they soften and start to color slightly.

3. **Make the sauce**
Stir in the tomato paste and let it cook briefly. Deglaze with the ouzo and
let the alcohol evaporate for a few minutes — the aroma that rises then is pure Greece.
Add the peeled tomatoes, bay leaf, oregano, pepper and a bit of salt.

4. **Let it simmer**
Return the fried sausage to the sauce, stir everything together well and let it simmer gently for about
30 minutes until the sauce is thick and fragrant. Taste and add a bit of
chili or Tabasco if you'd like extra heat.

## Serving & Storage Tips

- Serve spetsofai warm with fresh bread, to soak up the spiced sauce.
- A fresh Greek salad or a few pieces of feta go wonderfully with it. And if you want to go fully traditional: enjoy a small glass of well-chilled ouzo alongside it.
- The next day, reheated, spetsofai tastes even more intense — as if all the flavors have become one.
- Keeps for up to 3 days in the fridge. You can also freeze it in individual portions, ideal for a quick warm meal later.

## Ingredients

For 6 large portions or 8 smaller portions:

- 500 g dried white beans (e.g. gigante or cannellini beans)

- 3 celery stalks, cut into pieces

- 3 carrots, sliced

- 1 onion, finely chopped

- 1 to 2 tbsp tomato paste

- 3 bay leaves

- 1 stock cube (vegetable or chicken)

- Pepper and salt, to taste

- Olive oil (about 4 to 5 tbsp)

- About 2 liters water (a bit more if it gets too thick)

\*\*To serve:\*\*

- A good chunk of feta (crumbled into the soup or served alongside, as you prefer)

- A few drops of Tabasco, optional, for those who like some heat

- Fresh bread, preferably crusty and firm

## Preparation

\*\*The night before:\*\*

Rinse the beans well and soak them overnight in plenty of cold water. They absorb quite a lot of liquid, so use enough water.

\*\*The next day:\*\*

Drain the beans, rinse them again and add fresh water. Bring to a boil and let simmer gently for 10 minutes. Drain the water — this makes them lighter and easier to digest, and softer in flavor.

## Preparation

1. \*\*Cut the vegetables\*\*

Cut the onion and celery into small pieces, and the carrot preferably into slices or even half-slices.

2. \*\*Sauté the base\*\*

Add the onion, celery and carrot and let everything sauté gently for 5 to 7 minutes,

until the vegetables become fragrant and glossy. Stir in the tomato paste and let it cook briefly —

this gives a deeper, richer flavor. Start with about one and a half tablespoons of tomato paste;

when you add the water later, you can always add a bit more to taste.

3. \*\*Add the beans and seasoning\*\*

Add the beans to the pot, along with the bay leaves, the stock cube and the

water. Bring to a boil, reduce the heat and let simmer gently for about 1 to 1.5 hours

with the lid half on the pot.

4. \*\*Finishing touches\*\*

Once the beans are soft and the soup is thick and creamy, season with

pepper and salt. Too thick? Add a bit of hot water until it reaches the desired consistency.

## Serving & Storage Tips

- Fasolada is the kind of dish you eat with time and bread, not in a hurry. Serve the soup warm, with a drizzle of good olive oil on top.

- Almost every Greek table includes a block of feta — white and crumbly, as a counterbalance to the earthy sweetness of the beans. Some crumble the feta directly into their bowl, others place a piece alongside.

- And for those who like a bit of heat: a few drops of Tabasco bring the flavors to life without losing the character of the soup.

- The next day, once the flavors have fully settled, fasolada may taste even better than fresh from the pot.

- Keeps for up to 3 days in the fridge. You can also freeze it in individual portions, for whenever you're short on time.

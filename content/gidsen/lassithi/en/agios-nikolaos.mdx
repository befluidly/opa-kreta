## Agios Nikolaos and Surroundings – Eastern Crete at Its Best

\*Agios Nikolaos is one of the most pleasant small towns in East Crete. Located on the 
**Gulf of Mirabello**, it combines the charm of a harbor town with the calm of a small community. 
It's a place where you can easily spend a few days — not because there's one huge attraction, but 
because the whole package simply works.\*

### Lake Voulismeni

In the middle of town lies **Lake Voulismeni**, connected to the sea by a narrow channel. Around 
it you'll find cafés, terraces and small shops. According to legend, the lake is said to be 
bottomless, but in reality it's about 65 meters deep. It forms the natural heart of Agios 
Nikolaos, where locals and visitors meet.

<InfoBox title="Cliff Diving in Agios Nikolaos">
Every summer, the spectacular **Cliff Diving Event** takes place at the lake. Professional divers 
from around the world leap from the high rock walls into the deep water of Voulismeni. The event 
draws a large crowd and turns the town into a lively open-air theater full of music, sport and 
atmosphere for one weekend.
</InfoBox>

### The Harbor and Town Center

From the lake, it's just a few minutes' walk to the **harbor** and the **promenade**. Here, 
fishing boats, excursion boats and a handful of yachts sit side by side. The center is compact and 
easy to navigate, with pedestrian streets full of small shops, taverns and boutiques. The 
atmosphere is relaxed, especially outside high season.

### Beaches near Agios Nikolaos

There are several [beaches](/gidsen/natuur-en-wandelen/de-mooiste-stranden) within walking or 
short driving distance:

- **Kitroplatia Beach** – town beach near the center, with sunbeds and eateries.
- **Ammoudi Beach** – a bit smaller, popular with locals.
- **Almyros Beach** – a wide sandy beach on the southern edge of town, with shallow, clear water.
- **Voulisma Beach (Istron)** – a bit further east (about 12 km), with golden sand and turquoise 
sea, often named one of the most beautiful beaches in East Crete.

### Culture and Museums

The **Archaeological Museum of Agios Nikolaos** displays finds from across the region, including 
from the Minoan settlement of **Gournia**. That archaeological site lies a bit east of town and 
gives a good sense of what a Minoan trading settlement looked like three thousand years ago.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Konstantinou Palaiologou str. 41
**Opening hours:** Summer: Mon – Sun (closed Tue) from 08:30 to 15:30
**Admission:** €10 (full price)
</InfoBox>

In addition, the **Folk Art Museum** near the lake shows a modest but interesting collection of 
traditional objects and clothing.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Konstantinou Palaiologou str. 1
**Opening hours:** Summer, 14:00 to 18:30
</InfoBox>

### Elounda and the Sunken City of Olous

Twelve kilometers north of Agios Nikolaos lies **Elounda**, a former fishing village that has 
grown into a well-known resort town. Yet it has kept its calm character, especially outside the 
summer season.

Off the coast lie the remains of the **sunken city of Olous**, an ancient Greek settlement 
partially submerged due to earthquakes in antiquity. On calm days, you can still clearly see parts 
of the old walls, floor mosaics and columns in the clear water from the causeway. Olous was a 
prosperous city in antiquity, known for trade and shipbuilding, and is even mentioned in some 
theories as one of the possible locations of **Atlantis**.

Elounda gained international fame in the seventies through the BBC series _Who Pays the 
Ferryman?_, which was filmed here. That series first put the village on the map for British 
travelers — something you can still notice today from the loyal visitors who return year after 
year.

### Plaka and Spinalonga

A bit further along the coast lies **Plaka**, a small village with a view of the island of 
**Spinalonga**. From here, boats depart that cross the water to the island in just a few minutes — 
once a Venetian fortress, later used as a leper colony. The stone houses, narrow streets and 
remains of the fortress tell stories of isolation, but also of resilience and hope.

A visit to [Spinalonga](/gidsen/lassithi/spinalonga) combines well with lunch in Plaka, where 
several taverns along the quay serve fresh fish.

### Kritsa and the Mountains

Southwest of Agios Nikolaos lies the mountain village of **Kritsa**, known for its traditional 
atmosphere and craftsmanship. Narrow streets wind between white houses, and small workshops still 
produce woven fabrics and embroidery. Close to the village stands the **Church of Panagia Kera**, 
one of the most important Byzantine churches on Crete, with well-preserved frescoes from the 13th 
century.

### Practical Information

Agios Nikolaos lies about 65 kilometers east of Heraklion and is easily reached via the main road 
(E75). Parking is available on the edge of the center or near the harbor. The town is inhabited 
year-round, so many cafés, shops and restaurants stay open in spring and fall as well.

\*Crete is home to dozens of monasteries spread across its four regions: Chania, Rethymnon, Heraklion 
and Lassithi. Some are large and well-known, others small and remote, but each offers its own window 
into the island's religious and cultural life. Because of their locations — often in valleys, on 
hills or near gorges — they combine history with landscape, which makes them special places to 
visit.\*

\*This guide gives you an overview of the most important monasteries, along with a few remote 
chapels that stand out especially for their setting and views. Together they offer a varied picture 
of what Crete has to offer beyond the classic attractions.\*

## Moni Arkadi

Moni Arkadi is without doubt one of Crete's most emblematic monasteries. It sits on a plateau 
southeast of Rethymnon and is easily reached via a winding road through farmland. The complex is 
well-kept and easy to navigate, with a spacious courtyard and buildings dating largely from the 
Venetian period.

The monastery is best known for the events of 1866, during the Cretan uprising against Ottoman 
rule. Hundreds of people died in an explosion in the gunpowder magazine, a tragic event that turned 
Arkadi into a symbol of freedom. Various rooms display objects, documents and photographs from that 
period.

Despite its heavy history, a peaceful atmosphere prevails. Visitors often take their time to walk 
around and read the information boards. A visit takes about an hour on average.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Epar.Od. Viran Episkopis-Monis Arkadiou, Moni Arkadiou 741 50
**Region:** Rethymnon
**Opening hours:** Daily 09:00 – 20:00 | Wed: 13:00 – 20:00 (Last entry approximately 30 minutes before closing)
**Admission:** €4 per person, free up to age 16
</InfoBox>

## Moni Preveli

On the south coast, east of Plakias, lies Moni Preveli. The road there winds through a quiet 
valley, offering beautiful views of the surrounding hills along the way. The monastery consists of 
two parts:

- Kato Preveli (the old monastery), which consists mainly of ruins and historical elements
- Pisso Preveli, the active monastery you can visit today

A small museum holds icons, church silverware and photographs from 
[World War II](/en/gidsen/geschiedenis/kreta-in-de-tweede-wereldoorlog), when Allied soldiers found 
help here. Current monastic life takes place in the buildings around the courtyard.

The location is one of its greatest assets: from the hill you look out toward the sea and the 
valley that opens onto the famous [Preveli palm beach](/en/gidsen/natuur-en-wandelen/de-mooiste-stranden). 
Many travelers combine both attractions.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Επαρχία Αγίου Βασιλείου, Rethymno 741 50
**Region:** Rethymnon
**Opening hours:** Daily 09:00 - 13:30 and 15:30 - 18:30
**Admission:** €3 - €4 per person
</InfoBox>

## Selinari Monastery

Selinari Monastery lies in a green gorge along the road between Malia (Heraklion) and Neapoli 
(Lassithi). It's a small, active community with a strong bond to the local population. Many 
Cretans deliberately stop here while driving toward the east of the island.

The monastery is dedicated to Saint George. The main church is compact and surrounded by outbuildings, 
terraces and seating areas. Candles are often burning inside; outside you hear the sound of the 
gorge and the traffic passing by further down the road. The visit is short but meaningful — usually 
about fifteen minutes is enough.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Selinari 724 00
**Region:** Lassithi
**Opening hours:** Daily 09:00 – 12:00 and 15:00 to 18:00 (subject to change)
**Admission:** Not applicable – interior is not open to visitors
</InfoBox>

## Moni Kalivianni – Convent & Orphanage

In the [Messara Plain](/en/gidsen/heraklion/de-messaravlakte), not far from Moires, lies Moni 
Kalivianni. Unlike many old monasteries, this complex is known for its social character. For many 
years — and still today — it has served as a shelter for orphaned children and has also played a 
role in local education projects.

The grounds consist of modern and older buildings, a small church, workspaces and gardens. Because 
the monastery still has an active social function, it feels less touristy than other monasteries. 
The atmosphere is friendly and open, and visitors get a sense of how monasteries were, in recent 
history, part of the village communities of southern Heraklion.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Epar.Od. Miron - Pompias, Moni kalivianis 704 00
**Region:** Heraklion
**Opening hours:** Daily 07:00 – 19:00
**Admission:** Free, but you can donate via the website: https://www.pkocrete.com/
</InfoBox>

## Moni Toplou

Moni Toplou lies at the far eastern end of Crete, between Sitia and 
[Vai palm beach](/en/gidsen/lassithi/vai-beach). The monastery has a fortress-like appearance, with 
thick walls and a tall tower, since it was regularly attacked by pirates during the Venetian and 
Ottoman periods.

Inside you'll find a museum with icons, manuscripts and silverware. The monastery also produces 
[wine](/en/gidsen/eten-en-drinken/de-wijnhuizen-van-kreta) and 
[olive oil](/en/gidsen/eten-en-drinken/bezoek-olijfboeren), sold on-site. The complex's layout is 
modern and easy to navigate, with clear walking paths and information boards.

Toplou is a logical stop for anyone driving toward Vai, Itanos or the east coast.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Toplou 723 00
**Region:** Lassithi
**Opening hours:** Mon-Sat 9am-1pm and 2pm-6pm, museum closed on Sundays
**Admission:** €4 – wine tasting also available from €2.50 to €20
</InfoBox>

## Agia Triada

On the Akrotiri peninsula, northeast of [Chania](/en/gidsen/chania/musea-van-chania), lies Agia 
Triada, one of the best-preserved monasteries on the island. It was built in the 17th century by 
monks of Venetian descent, which shows in the symmetrical architecture: a wide courtyard, clean 
lines and a central domed church.

The monastery is still inhabited by monks who maintain the surrounding olive groves. In the 
monastery shop, you can buy products made on-site, such as olive oil, wine and honey.

The peaceful location and beautiful architecture make Agia Triada a pleasant stop during a visit 
to the beaches and viewpoints of Akrotiri.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Akrotiri, Chania
**Region:** Chania
**Opening hours:** Daily 08:00 – 19:30
**Admission:** €3 (adult), €1.50 (reduced), free up to age 16
</InfoBox>

## Kera Kardiotissa

On the route to the Lassithi Plateau lies Kera Kardiotissa, a small monastery known for an icon 
that, according to legend, was stolen multiple times and each time "returned" on its own. Whether 
historically accurate or not, the story has given the monastery a certain reputation.

The complex is compact, with a church full of frescoes, a small monastery shop and shaded terraces. 
The surroundings are mountainous and calming. Many travelers take a short break here before 
continuing on toward the windmills and villages of the plateau.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Panagia Kera, Pediados
**Region:** Lassithi
**Opening hours:** Daily 7:00 to 19:00
**Admission:** No information available – possibly free
</InfoBox>

## Moni Odigitria

In the hills of southern Heraklion, on the way to the Agiofarago 
[gorge](/en/gidsen/natuur-en-wandelen/de-mooiste-kloven), lies Moni Odigitria. It's an old monastery 
with thick stone walls and a courtyard that feels a bit like a small fortress. Here you'll find, 
among other things, a watchtower, a simple church and a small exhibit of everyday objects from 
earlier centuries.

In 1866, during the Cretan uprising and under the abbotship of Gerasimos Manidakis, the monastery 
played an important role in the resistance. That involvement led to harsh reprisals from the 
Ottoman authorities, who damaged the monastery and used violence against the community. During the 
German occupation too, the monastery remained active in supporting resistance fighters. That again 
led to hostility and pressure from the occupiers, since the monastery provided shelter and help to 
people involved in the resistance.

The surroundings are rural and peaceful. Because of its proximity to the Agiofarago Gorge, the 
monastery is often combined with a walk toward the beach further along the gorge.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Faistos, 704 00
**Region:** Heraklion
**Opening hours:** Daily 07:00 –20:00
**Admission:** No information available – possibly free
</InfoBox>

## Remote Chapels with a View

Alongside the monasteries, Crete is home to countless small chapels. Many sit on hills, in gorges 
or in spots that don't immediately jump out on a map, but offer a beautiful view or a special 
atmosphere. Below are three recommendations that fit nicely into a route along the monasteries.

### Agios Panteleimonas (Prinias)

Above the village of Prinias sits the chapel of Agios Panteleimonas, in a spot with open views 
over the interior of the island. The church is small and usually closed, but the surrounding area 
is accessible. The drive up is short, and it's a pleasant stop on a clear day.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** <a href="https://maps.app.goo.gl/q1qKLAKK4RPu5fpe6" target="_blank">Google Maps</a>
**Region:** Heraklion
**Opening hours:** Chapel is often closed, but the view is phenomenal
**Admission:** Not applicable
</InfoBox>

### Agios Paisios – Damnoni/Ammoudi

This small stone chapel sits on a hill between Damnoni and Ammoudi Beach. The building consists of 
rough masonry in earthy tones and fits well into the open landscape.

There are two ways to reach the chapel:

- On foot: via a steep path from Damnoni
- By car: via an unpaved road toward Ammoudi, where you can park right below the chapel

From the top, you get a panoramic view over the bays of Damnoni and the hills around Plakias. It 
can get quite windy up here. The chapel is usually open, and inside there's a calm, understated 
atmosphere. It's a nice, short stop along a drive on the south coast.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** <a href="https://maps.app.goo.gl/arqUa2UtEX1h7sKc7" target="_blank">By car – unpaved road!</a> - <a href="https://maps.app.goo.gl/eG1AtHbv2zLdAGGi9" target="_blank">On foot from Damnoni</a>
**Region:** Rethymnon
**Opening hours:** No information found.
**Admission:** Not applicable
</InfoBox>

### Osios Nikolaos Kourtaliotis

Deep in the Kourtaliotiko Gorge lies the chapel of Osios Nikolaos Kourtaliotis. You reach it via 
stairs descending from the road, passing views of the steep rock walls and the flowing water of the 
gorge along the way.

The chapel is small and simple, but its location makes it special. Inside it's cool and quiet, 
while the natural sounds of the gorge remain in the background. The church is often visited by 
hikers exploring the gorge or on their way to Preveli.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** <a href="https://maps.app.goo.gl/w78Rk8d47kKY2gUz6" target="_blank">Google Maps</a>
**Region:** Rethymnon
**Opening hours:** Daily 08:00 – 19:30, closed Sundays (last entry)
**Admission:** €5 (adult)
</InfoBox>

## One Last Note

The monasteries and remote chapels of Crete show just how diverse the island is. Some tell stories 
of struggle and history, others show the daily rhythm of monks or the connection between religion 
and landscape. Together they make a wonderful addition to a road trip across the island, especially 
for travelers wanting to discover Crete's quieter, more cultural side.

Visitors are expected to dress respectfully when entering an Orthodox monastery or church. Shorts 
and/or bare shoulders are generally not appreciated — not out of strictness, but because many 
Greeks remain deeply religious and maintain these places with great devotion. Priests, monks or 
nuns are often still present, and modest attire is seen as a simple but meaningful gesture of 
respect.

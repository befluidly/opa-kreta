TITEL:

\\*Museums and archaeological sites on Crete can add up quickly if you're planning to 

visit several during your trip. What many travelers don't realize: on a set number of 

days each year, admission to all state-run museums and sites on the island is 

completely free. Here's the full overview, so you can plan your visit wisely.\\*

## Why Does Free Admission Exist?

All archaeological sites and museums under the Greek Ministry of Culture follow the 

same national policy for free admission days. That applies to every state site on 

Crete — from [Knossos](/gidsen/heraklion/knossos) to the 

[Archaeological Museum of Heraklion](/gidsen/heraklion/musea-van-heraklion) — but 

\*\*not\*\* to privately managed museums (such as the Folklore Museum of Chania or smaller 

private collections), which set their own pricing.

## The Free Admission Days of 2026

- \*\*March 6\*\* — Melina Mercouri Memorial Day

- \*\*April 18\*\* — International Monuments Day

- \*\*May 18\*\* — International Museum Day

- \*\*Last weekend of September\*\* — European Heritage Days

- \*\*October 28\*\* — Greek National Holiday

- \*\*The first and third Sunday of each month\*\*, from November 1 through March 31 

(low season)

Keep in mind: these days can be busier than usual, especially at well-known sites 

like Knossos. If you're specifically after a quiet visit, it's better to plan for a 

regular day instead.

## Which Sites on Crete Participate?

All state-run museums and sites fall under this policy. The best-known ones on Crete:

### Heraklion

- [Palace of Knossos](/gidsen/heraklion/knossos)

- [Archaeological Museum of Heraklion](/gidsen/heraklion/musea-van-heraklion)

- Historical Museum of Crete (note: this is partly privately managed — check on-site)

### Chania

- [Museums in Chania](/gidsen/chania/musea-van-chania) — the Archaeological Museum and 

the Byzantine Collection follow the national policy; the Maritime Museum and the 

Folklore Museum are privately run and don't participate

### Lassithi

- Archaeological Museum of Agios Nikolaos

- [Spinalonga](/gidsen/lassithi/spinalonga)

- Palace of Kato Zakros

### Rethymno

- The Fortezza and the Archaeological Museum of Rethymno

## Practical Tips

- \*\*Always check the official Ministry of Culture website beforehand\*\* (or at the 

ticket counter on-site) — dates and policies can change slightly from year to year.

- \*\*Free admission doesn't include guided tours or audio guides\*\* — those usually 

still come with a fee, even on free days.

- \*\*Plan smart\*\*: if you're already traveling during winter (November–March), there 

are two free Sundays every month — a great way to fit in a visit to Knossos or the 

Archaeological Museum.

- \*\*Already free year-round\*\*: visitors up to age 25 with EU nationality, and 

visitors up to age 18 from outside the EU, don't pay admission at most state sites to 

begin with — be sure to bring an ID.

## In Short

If you're already planning a visit to Knossos, the Archaeological Museum, or 

Spinalonga, it's worth checking whether your travel date happens to fall on one of 

these free days. Saving a few euros is a nice bonus — just expect a bit more of a 

crowd than usual.

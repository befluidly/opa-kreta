\*If there are two names that come up every time someone googles "most beautiful beaches Crete," 
it's **Balos** and **Elafonissi**. Turquoise water, white-pink sand, thousands of photos on 
Instagram — and, let's be honest, also thousands of people trying to take that exact same photo 
at the same time as you. This guide gives you the full practical rundown: how to get there, what 
it costs, when to go, and whether the hype is justified.\*

## Balos Lagoon

### What makes Balos special?

Balos lies at the very northwestern tip of Crete, at the end of the Gramvousa peninsula. It's a 
lagoon: shallow, turquoise water flowing into deeper blue, with a view of the small island of 
Gramvousa and its Venetian fort. The combination of colors and its remote location make Balos one 
of the most photographed spots in Greece.

### How do you get to Balos?

There are two ways, and they differ quite a bit.

**By car (via the dirt road)**
From the village of Kaliviani, an unpaved, steep dirt road runs about 12 kilometers toward the 
coast. This is manageable with a regular [rental car](/en/praktisch/auto-huren-op-kreta), but expect a slow, bumpy drive. At the end, 
you park on a plateau — and then comes a 20 to 30 minute descent on foot to the beach itself. The 
climb back up, in full sun, is tougher than the way down.

<InfoBox title="Entrance Fee">
At Kaliviani, you'll pay a small fee (around €1) toward the upkeep of the Gramvousa nature 
reserve and the road. It's a good idea to bring cash.
</InfoBox>

**By boat from Kissamos**
Excursion boats leave daily from the port of Kissamos (Kastelli), combining a visit to Balos with 
a stop at the island of Gramvousa (with its Venetian fort). Expect to pay around €25–30 per adult, 
plus a small municipal fee of €1 per person (ages 13+) on top of the ticket price. These boats 
usually arrive around 11:30am and leave again by 4:30pm — so that window is the busiest time on 
the beach.

### When's the best time to go?

- **Early in the morning**, before the boats from Kissamos arrive (so before 11am)
- **Late in the afternoon**, after they've left (after 4:30pm)
- Outside of July-August, Balos is generally much quieter anyway

### Facilities

At the beach you'll find a limited number of sunbeds and umbrellas (rental), a small stand/café 
and restrooms. Swimming is allowed on the beach itself, but not in the protected lagoon further on.

## Elafonissi Beach

### What makes Elafonissi special?

Elafonissi lies at the very southwestern tip of Crete and is famous for its light pink sand — 
formed from crushed shells and coral fragments — and a shallow lagoon that's perfect for wading. 
A small, uninhabited islet is part of the site: at low tide you can wade right across to it, and 
on its far side there's a second, quieter beach.

**Honest expectation:** the "pink" sand isn't equally vivid everywhere — that depends heavily on 
the light and which part of the beach you're standing on. In some spots it's clearly visible, in 
others it's barely distinguishable from regular white sand.

### How do you get to Elafonissi?

Unlike Balos, the road to Elafonissi is **fully paved**, all the way to the parking area. A bus is 
available from Chania, and by car you're looking at about 1.5 to 2 hours from Chania town. No 
walking descent needed — you step almost directly onto the beach.

<InfoBox title="Practical Info">
**Water:** shallow, ideal for kids  
**Sunbeds/umbrellas:** available, expect around €10–15 per set in high season  
**Protected area:** Elafonissi falls under Natura 2000 — stay on the marked paths and respect the dunes
</InfoBox>

### When's the best time to go?

Elafonissi is one of the busiest beaches on Crete in July and August. Arrive early in the morning 
to find a quiet spot and secure a sunbed, or come right before sunset when most day-trippers have 
already left.

## Balos vs. Elafonissi: Which One Should You Choose?

| | Balos | Elafonissi |
|---|---|---|
| Accessibility | Dirt road + walk, or boat | Fully paved, easy |
| Suitable with kids | Less so (descent, no shade) | Very suitable (shallow water) |
| Atmosphere | Wilder, more remote | More accessible, busier |
| Facilities | Limited | More extensive |

**My honest opinion:** if you can only do one of the two and are physically up for the walk, 
choose Balos — the remote location and the view of Gramvousa make it just that bit more special. 
If you're traveling with young kids or want to swim without any hassle, Elafonissi is the more 
practical choice.

## Is It Worth the Hype?

Honestly? Both places are beautiful, but neither is the "hidden paradise" some photos suggest. In 
high season, you'll be sharing the beach with hundreds of other visitors. Come outside of 
July-August, or early in the day, and you'll actually get something close to those dreamy images.

Elafonissi, but with a fraction of the crowds.

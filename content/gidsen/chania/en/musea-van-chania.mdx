*Chania is a city of many faces. The sea breathes salt and light here, the alleyways 
smell of coffee and orange blossom, and everywhere you see traces of civilizations 
that followed one another. To get to know the heart of this city, you don't have to 
look far: the museums of Chania hold the island's memory, from Minoan times to 
modern Greece.*

## Archaeological Museum of Chania

The Archaeological Museum of Chania is without doubt the city's showpiece. Since 
2022, it has been housed in a modern building in Chalepa, the elegant neighborhood 
just above the center. The architecture is bright and spacious: lots of glass, lots 
of light, and an open layout that brings the finds to life.

Inside awaits a journey through time — from the Neolithic to the Roman period. You'll 
see Minoan vases, bronze weapons, mosaics, amulets and jewelry. Each object seems to 
whisper something about daily life thousands of years ago.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Skra 15, Chalepa, Chania 731 33
**Opening hours:** Summer (April 1 – October 31): 08:00 – 20:00, closed on Tuesdays | Winter (November 1 – March 31): 08:30 – 15:30, closed on Tuesdays (Last entry approx. 20 min. before closing)
**Admission:** €15 (full price) / €8 (reduced) (Prices and hours are subject to change — see amch.gr for up-to-date information.)
</InfoBox>

## Maritime Museum of Crete

At the entrance to the Venetian Harbor, inside the Firka Fortress, lies the Maritime 
Museum of Crete. The scent of sea and wood is never far away. Inside you'll find 
models of old ships, navigation instruments, maps, uniforms and photographs telling 
the island's maritime history.

The museum depicts not only war and trade, but also the bond between Cretans and 
the sea — a relationship of pride and survival. On the ground floor you'll find 
Minoan seafaring, while upstairs covers more modern times as well. Outside, at the 
Moro shipyard, there's even a reconstruction of a Minoan ship that was once 
actually launched.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Firka Fortress, Venetian Harbor of Chania
**Opening hours:** Daily 09:00 – 16:00 (shorter in winter, longer in summer)
**Admission:** €4 (adults) / €2 (reduced) (Check current hours at mar-mus-crete.gr)
</InfoBox>

## Folklore Museum of Chania

Tucked away in the old town, right next to the Catholic church on Halidon Street, 
lies the Folklore Museum of Chania — small, warm and charming. Here you won't find 
marble statues or gilded frames, but the simple life of the past: handicrafts, 
looms, household items, traditional costumes and wooden tools.

You walk through a reconstructed Cretan house, complete with a kitchen corner, a 
bridal chamber and small workshops. Everything breathes authenticity. Sometimes 
someone is even sitting there embroidering — a quiet reminder that tradition here 
is not a dead letter.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Halidon Street, Old Town of Chania
**Opening hours:** Daily 10:00 – 18:00
**Admission:** €3 (Check locally for updates — the museum is privately managed)
</InfoBox>

## Eleftherios Venizelos Museum of Chalepa

In the green Chalepa neighborhood, just above Chania, lies the former home of 
Eleftherios Venizelos (1864–1936) — one of modern Greece's most important 
statesmen. The house is now a museum, lovingly preserved by the Venizelos Foundation.

Inside, you walk through rooms full of personal belongings, letters, uniforms and 
photographs. You can feel the spirit of a man who guided Crete and Greece through a 
turbulent century. Outside, the garden offers a view over the bay, peaceful and still.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Chalepa, Chania 731 33
**Opening hours:** Tue–Sun 09:00 – 17:00 (may vary)
**Admission:** €4 (Check with the Venizelos Foundation for current information.)
</InfoBox>

## Byzantine and Post-Byzantine Collection of Chania

In the old San Salvatore Church, close to the western side of the Venetian Harbor, 
lies the Byzantine and Post-Byzantine Collection of Chania. The atmosphere here is 
subdued: thick walls, soft light, and icons that seem to still carry the scent of 
incense.

The collection displays art and religious objects from the early and later Byzantine 
period — mosaics, wall fragments, crucifixes, pottery and jewelry. Much of it comes 
from churches in West Crete and offers a unique glimpse into the island's religious 
history.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** San Salvatore Church, Theotokopoulou Street, Chania 731 00
**Opening hours:** Tue–Sun 08:30 – 15:30
**Admission:** €4 (Check current information via the West Crete Antiquities Service)
</InfoBox>

## A City of Memories

Chania is more than its harbor and alleyways — it's a city of stories, carefully 
preserved behind wooden doors and in stone halls. Each museum tells a different 
facet: ancient Crete, the sea, domestic life, political struggle and faith.

Visit them with time and attention, and you'll find that Chania isn't just a place 
to see, but one to feel.

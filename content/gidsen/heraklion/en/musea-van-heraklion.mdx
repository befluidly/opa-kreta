\*Heraklion has many faces. The bustle of the market, the calls of fishermen at the harbor, 
the scent of coffee and thyme in the narrow streets — but behind that lively present hides a 
city that proudly preserves its past. Anyone who spends a day in Heraklion discovers that the 
city is really one big open-air museum. Yet there are places where time speaks even more 
quietly: the museums. There, in the coolness of stone halls and among carefully lit display 
cases, lies the heart of Crete's history, nature and soul.\*

## Heraklion Archaeological Museum

The Archaeological Museum is without doubt the beating heart of Heraklion — and for many, 
of all of Crete. Here the Minoan civilization, the oldest in Europe, comes back to life. The 
moment you step into the halls, it feels as though you're walking through thousands of years 
of history: from the earliest settlements to the refined palaces of 
[Knossos](/gidsen/heraklion/knossos), Phaistos and Malia. The display cases gleam with colorful 
frescoes, gold jewelry and mysterious figurines of goddesses holding snakes in their hands. The 
famous Phaistos Disc is here, small but charged with mystery, while the dancing figures on 
pottery still seem to hold movement within them. The rooms are spacious, bright and modern — 
none of the dusty image some museums carry. The atmosphere is serene; only the soft tap of 
heels and the whisper of visitors fill the space.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Xanthoudidou & Hatzidaki 1, 712 02 Heraklion
**Opening hours:** Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun: 08:00 – 20:00 | Wed: 13:00 – 20:00 (Last entry approximately 30 minutes before closing)
**Admission:** €12 (full price), €6 (reduced) (Prices and hours are subject to change — check heraklionmuseum.gr for current information.)
</InfoBox>

## Historical Museum of Crete

The moment you step into the elegant neoclassical building on the coast, you notice the 
difference from the Archaeological Museum. No pottery shards or Minoan goddess frescoes here, 
but paintings, maps, documents and stories of people from more recent centuries.

The museum tells the story of Crete after antiquity — from Byzantium to Venetian and Ottoman 
rule, and finally the modern Greek state. In each room you feel the island's shifting identity: 
Byzantine icons, Venetian altarpieces, old weapons, photographs of resistance fighters.

On the upper floor hangs, in fact, a small but striking painting by El Greco — born on Crete, 
long before he became world-famous in Spain.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** 27 Sofokli Venizelou Ave. / 7 Lysimachou Kalokerinou St., 71202 Heraklion
**Opening hours:** Summer (April – October): Mon–Sun 09:00 – 17:00 | Winter (Nov – March): Mon–Fri 09:00 – 15:30, Sat 10:00 – 16:00, closed Sundays
**Admission:** €8 (summer), €5 (winter) – discounts available for students and seniors (Check current information via historical-museum.gr)
</InfoBox>

## Natural History Museum of Crete

On the coastal road, in an old industrial building that once housed a power plant, lies a 
completely different kind of museum. The Natural History Museum shows the wild, rugged side of 
Crete: the mountains, fossils, flowers, animals and seas that bring the island to life.

As you enter, you're greeted by the skeleton of a whale — immense, almost otherworldly. Further 
along, you walk past dioramas of forests, mountain slopes and 
[gorges](/gidsen/natuur-en-wandelen/de-mooiste-kloven), with mounted animals in natural poses. 
Kids never tire of the interactive corner, where you can "feel" earthquakes in the seismic 
simulator or see live lizards and fish in the "Living Museum."

The rooms are modern and well-maintained, with friendly staff happy to explain. For anyone who's 
had enough of ruins and stone statues for a while, this is a refreshing dive into another 
dimension of Crete.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Sofokli Venizelou Ave., Heraklion (near the harbor)
**Opening hours:** Mon–Fri: 09:00 – 17:00 | Sat–Sun: 10:00 – 18:00
**Admission:** Approximately €7.50 (adult), €4.50 (reduced) (Prices subject to change — see nhmc.uoc.gr)
</InfoBox>

## Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology

One of the most surprising museums in Heraklion is housed in a Venetian building in the old 
center. The Kotsanas Museum brings the genius of the ancient Greeks to life with replicas of 
their inventions: automatic doors, water devices, musical instruments, mechanical birds — and 
even the first "robot servant." The museum isn't large, but the enthusiasm is contagious. The 
guides demonstrate how gears, pulleys and hydraulics were already in use thousands of years ago. 
Suddenly antiquity no longer feels distant, but bursting with imagination and ingenuity.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Epimenidou 18, Heraklion
**Opening hours:** Daily 10:00 – 18:00 (summer) | daily 09:00 – 15:00 (winter)
**Admission:** Around €12 (check on-site) (More info via kotsanasmuseum.com/crete)
</InfoBox>

## Museum of Christian Art – St. Catherine of Sinai

Right near St. Minas Cathedral, in the heart of Heraklion, stands the old church of St. 
Catherine — now a small museum full of religious art. Inside it's cool and quiet; only the soft 
gleam of gold leaf on icons lights the space. The collection contains Byzantine paintings, 
priestly vestments, manuscripts and crosses — carefully preserved in the shadow of centuries-old 
stone walls.

What makes this museum special is its atmosphere: simple, devout and sincere. You don't have to 
be religious to feel the beauty. The icons speak of a deep stillness, of faith and endurance 
that has carried Crete through the centuries.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Monis Odigitrias 1, Heraklion
**Opening hours:** Mon–Sat 9:30–19:30 | Sun 10:30–19:30
**Admission:** Approximately €4 (Check on-site or via visitheraklion.eu)
</InfoBox>

## Video Games Museum

Among the centuries-old stones and churches of Heraklion, you might not expect it, but right in 
the middle of the [city](/gidsen/heraklion/bezoek-heraklion) there's also a small museum devoted 
to something much younger: the world of video games. The Video Games Museum is a cheerful 
tribute to the digital nostalgia of the '80s, '90s and 2000s. Old consoles, cartridges, 
joysticks — everything still works, and visitors are often welcome to play themselves. It's 
small, charming and the perfect light-hearted stop between the heavier museums.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** 20 Pediados Street, 71201, Heraklion
**Opening hours:** Daily 08:00 – 19:30, closed Sundays (last entry)
**Admission:** €10 (adult), €7 (reduced)
</InfoBox>

## A City Full of Stories

[Heraklion](/gidsen/heraklion/bezoek-heraklion) is not a city that hides its past. It carries it, 
visible in its walls, in its people, and in its museums. Each museum is a different window onto 
Crete: one shows antiquity, another nature, struggle, or faith. Together they form a mosaic of 
memory and renewal — and anyone who visits them discovers that the island offers not just sun 
and sea, but also stillness, depth and wonder. Anyone who truly wants to understand Heraklion 
shouldn't just see her museums, but feel them. And that begins — like every beautiful discovery — 
with pausing for a moment.

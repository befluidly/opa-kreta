## Day Trip from Chersonissos – Malia, Lyrakis, Milatos Cave and Milatos Beach

### Chersonissos

Chersonissos (Limenas Chersonissou) is one of the most accessible bases along Crete's north 
coast. From the harbor, you're on the old national road toward Malia within minutes. The route 
is simple and generally avoids the busier new highway.

This day trip combines archaeology, local production and a relaxed coastal stop. Everything is 
relatively close together, keeping the day calm and easy to follow.

### The Minoan Palace Complex of Malia

About a fifteen-minute drive away lies the archaeological site of Malia, one of Crete's most 
important [Minoan sites](/gidsen/heraklion/knossos). The site is large enough to be interesting, 
but small enough to stay easy to navigate. You'll walk among the foundations of houses, storage 
rooms, workshops and the remains of the palace complex. Many areas are clearly marked with signs 
in English and Greek.

The visit is at your own pace. There's little shade, so water and sun protection are essential. 
Parking is free and usually not overcrowded, even in high season. An hour to an hour and a half 
is enough to comfortably see the site.

### Lyrakis Olive Oil Factory

From Malia, you continue further inland to the Lyrakis factory. It's a small family business 
that combines modern machinery with traditional working methods. The tour usually starts with a 
brief explanation of the olive harvest, the pressing process, and the differences in oil quality.

Afterward, you get the chance to 
[taste](/gidsen/eten-en-drinken/bezoek-olijfboeren) several oils, often alongside small pieces of 
bread. The staff clearly explain how to recognize good olive oil and what terms like "extra 
virgin" and "cold-pressed" actually mean. The shop is modest but well-stocked, with oils, herbs 
and local products. Plan for about half an hour here.

### Milatos Cave (Spilia Milatou)

The road to Milatos Cave winds up into the hills and remains easy to drive. The cave lies a few 
hundred meters above the village and is reached via a short walk from the parking area. The path 
is uneven but not difficult.

The cave itself consists of several chambers, the first of which is the largest. Here stands a 
small chapel, dedicated to Agios Thomas. Further in are a few smaller rooms. The cave is 
historically significant: in 1823, hundreds of local residents took temporary shelter here during 
an Ottoman attack. Part of that history is noted on-site.

A flashlight or phone light comes in handy, as it's fairly dark inside. The stop takes 30 to 40 
minutes in total.

### Milatos Beach – Lunch & Harbor

After the cave, you drive down to Milatos Beach, a quiet, low-key coastal spot where tourism 
stays small-scale. The [beach](/gidsen/natuur-en-wandelen/de-mooiste-stranden) consists of pebbles 
and a few sandy stretches. The sea is often clear and fresh, and the small harbor gives the whole 
place a relaxed feel.

For lunch, there are two good taverns along the coastal road:

- **Akrogiali** – a classic seafood restaurant with simple, fresh dishes.
- **Meraklis** – friendly, approachable and popular with locals.

Both sit right on the coastal road and have shaded terraces. After lunch, you can take a relaxed 
walk toward the small harbor. Here you'll find the No Name Bar, a simple but well-loved spot for 
coffee or a soft drink. The atmosphere is easygoing, with no loud music or busy terraces.

For those with time to spare, a quick swim or half an hour by the water makes for a nice way to 
end the stop. Milatos Beach isn't a classic "beach day" beach, but it's ideal for a short, quiet 
break.

### Drive Back to Chersonissos

From Milatos, you drive back to Chersonissos either via Sisi or again via Malia. Both routes take 
about the same time (35–40 minutes). By late afternoon, the road around Malia gets busier, so if 
you're after a calmer drive, take the inland route via Sisi.

### Practical Information

- **Total distance**: 45–55 km (round trip)
- **Driving time**: 1.5 hours excluding stops
- **Recommended time**: half a day to a full day
- **Suitable for**: families, relaxed travelers, lovers of archaeology and small coastal villages

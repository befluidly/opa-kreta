\*Anyone traveling across Crete today sees olive groves, mountain villages and quiet gorges. Yet 
those same places are still marked by the events of May 1941, when the island became the setting 
for one of the fiercest battles of World War II. The German airborne invasion of Crete — Operation 
Merkur — began on May 20, 1941, and ended in a bloody victory for the Germans. The island paid an 
especially heavy price for it.\*

## The Battle of Crete and Its Aftermath

The invasion began at Maleme, on the north coast west of Chania. Thousands of German paratroopers 
landed among olive groves and villages, but many never reached the ground alive: they were killed 
in the air or shortly after landing by Cretan civilians and Allied troops. The resistance was fierce 
and spontaneous — Cretans fought with whatever they had: hunting rifles, knives, even farming tools. 
The German response, however, was merciless. After the island fell, reprisals followed against 
civilians: villages were burned to the ground, men executed, families wiped out. The atrocities in 
Kandanos, Viannos and Anogia rank among the darkest chapters in Crete's history. At the same time, 
residents helped wherever they could: wounded soldiers were hidden, and in the weeks following the 
defeat, Allied troops tried to flee to the south coast through mountain gorges such as the Imbros 
Gorge, the Kallikrati Gorge and the Aradena Gorge, often via Chora Sfakion, where evacuations by sea 
took place.

## German War Cemetery at Maleme

Above the village of Maleme, on a low hill overlooking the bay of Chania, lies the German military 
cemetery of Maleme. Here rest more than 4,400 German soldiers who died during the airborne invasion 
and the fighting of May 1941. The site is sober and peaceful: no white crosses, but flat, 
dark-gray stones set in the grass. The simplicity underscores the anonymity and scale of the loss. 
From the cemetery, you look out over the sea and the old Maleme airfield — a striking panorama that 
recalls the drama that unfolded here.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Maleme, 18 km west of Chania
**Opening hours:** Daily from sunrise to sunset
**Admission:** Free
</InfoBox>

## Commonwealth War Cemetery – Souda Bay

East of Chania, on the quiet bay of Souda, lies the Commonwealth War Cemetery. Here rest 1,527 
servicemen from the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand, who died during the Battle of Crete 
or in captivity. The cemetery is exceptionally well maintained: straight rows of white headstones, 
olive trees and views of the bright blue sea. At its center stands the Cross of Sacrifice, as found 
at many British war cemeteries. The place carries a quiet sense of simplicity, reverence and 
stillness.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Souda Bay, 5 km east of Chania
**Opening hours:** Daily 08:00 – 17:00
**Admission:** Free
</InfoBox>

## Memorials of Remembrance

### Kandanos War Memorial

In the village of Kandanos, south of Chania, stands a moving memorial to the villagers executed in 
June 1941. The original village was completely destroyed by the Germans in reprisal for resistance 
activity. Today, a simple plaque commemorates that tragedy.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Kandanos, Chania region
**Opening hours:** Always accessible
**Admission:** Free
</InfoBox>

### Amiras Memorial (Viannos)

South of Heraklion, above the Lassithi plain, lies the Viannos–Amiras Memorial. It commemorates 
the mass killing of September 1943, in which more than 400 residents were executed. The modern 
monument overlooks the sea and is one of Crete's most important war memorials.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Amiras, Viannos region (Heraklion)
**Opening hours:** Always accessible
**Admission:** Free
</InfoBox>

### Memorial for the Greek Cadets

Near Chania, on the grounds of the former military academy, this monument honors the young Greek 
cadets who died defending the island — a lesser-known but essential chapter of the battle.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Chania Military Academy grounds
**Opening hours:** By request / limited access
**Admission:** Free
</InfoBox>

### Maleme Airfield Memorial

Close to the cemetery, on the grounds of the old airfield, stands the Maleme Airfield Memorial. It 
marks the strategic point where the outcome of the battle was decided.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Old Maleme airfield
**Opening hours:** Limited access – permission from the air force base required
**Admission:** Free
</InfoBox>

### Monument to the Battle of Crete – Irakleio

In [Irakleio](/gidsen/heraklion/bezoek-heraklion), along the coastal road, you'll find the modern 
Monument to the Battle of Crete, erected in honor of all fallen Cretans and Allied soldiers. Less 
visited, but symbolically important to the island's collective memory.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Irakleio, coastal road near the harbor
**Opening hours:** Always accessible
**Admission:** Free
</InfoBox>

## War Museums on Crete

### Historical Memorial of Maleme Airfield

Right by the old Maleme airfield lies a small museum run by the Greek Air Force. Inside you'll find 
aircraft parts, weapons, maps and photographs from May 1941.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Maleme, Chania 73014
**Opening hours:** Mon–Fri 10:00 – 13:00 (by appointment)
**Admission:** Free – contact in advance via maleme.115cw@haf.gr
</InfoBox>

### War Museum Askyfou – Sfakia

In the mountain village of Askyfou, you'll find a private war museum with hundreds of objects: 
helmets, weapons, documents and parachutes. An authentic place full of local involvement.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Askyfou, Sfakia region
**Opening hours:** Daily 10:00 – 18:00
**Admission:** €3 p.p.
</InfoBox>

### World War 2 Museum – Platanias

In Platanias, a small museum is housed in a former German bunker. The underground tunnels display 
uniforms, weapons and personal belongings of soldiers and residents. Simple but moving.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Platanias, near Chania
**Opening hours:** Daily 10:00 – 17:00
**Admission:** €3 p.p.
</InfoBox>

### Chromonastirion Military Museum

In the Rethymnon region lies the regional branch of Athens's national war museum. Compact but 
informative, with documents, uniforms and photographs from the Battle of Crete and the resistance.

<InfoBox>
**Location:** Chromonastiri 741 50, Rethymnon
**Opening hours:** Daily 09:00 – 19:00 (closed on public holidays)
**Admission:** €3 (adults) – €2 (65+) – free up to age 18
</InfoBox>

## Tip: Read the Book Crete 1941–1945 by Antony Beevor

Anyone wanting to dive deeper into the events of the Battle of Crete and the occupation that 
followed shouldn't miss the book _Crete: The Battle and the Resistance_ by British historian Antony 
Beevor. The book combines military precision with human stories — from the German airborne troops 
to the Cretan resistance fighters and the British soldiers who tried to escape to the coast through 
the gorges. Beevor drew on diaries, letters and archival material, bringing the dramatic days of May 
1941 to life like no one else. A gripping and accessible book for anyone who wants not just to see 
Crete, but to understand it.

## Me Raki Taverna – Our Discovery Just Outside Chersonissos

\*We've been coming to Chersonissos for more than 25 years, and yet we only discovered Me Raki 
Taverna a few years ago. Friends from Antwerp, who like us are regular visitors to Crete, took us 
there. Apparently we'd been driving past it all that time without realizing what kind of place it 
was. It sits just outside town, on the road toward the highway. A five- to ten-minute drive, and if 
you don't know it's there, you'll pass right by it. But now that we know it, it's become a regular 
stop.\*

### What Does "Me Raki" Mean?

The name Me Raki (με ράκι) actually has a double meaning. Literally, it means "with raki" — a nod 
to the Cretan spirit that flows generously here — but at the same time it refers to meraki (μεράκι), 
a Greek word roughly meaning to do something with love, passion and feeling. If someone cooks me 
meraki, they're doing it with their heart.

And that sums up this taverna perfectly: here you taste both the raki and the soul behind the 
cooking.

### Not Your Typical Taverna

Me Raki doesn't look like the classic Greek taverna you'd picture. It's an open-air place, with 
just one wall and a terrace offering open views on every side. Flower pots with flowers and herbs 
are scattered everywhere, and the chairs are all different colors: blue, red, yellow, green… nothing 
really matches, but that's exactly what gives it its charm.

Inside, it's simple and relaxed. No kitsch, no decor trying too hard to look Greek — just a place 
where people come to eat, talk and laugh.

### The Menu: Small Dishes with Big Flavors

At Me Raki, you don't order from a thick menu. You get a single A4 sheet full of mezes, small 
dishes meant for sharing. You mark what you want, hand the sheet to Michalis or Alexis, and then the 
feast begins.

The dishes are purely Cretan. Here you'll still find classic mezes that have become rare 
elsewhere: liver, snails, apaki (smoked pork), loukaniko (Cretan sausage), dakos, and the 
traditional pasta [skioufichta](/recepten/skioufichta) — not to be confused with kritharaki, which 
is Greek, not Cretan.

You can both lunch and dine here. During the day it's quiet, with locals taking a short break. In 
the evening it fills up quickly, especially in summer. There are only about twenty tables, so a 
reservation is definitely not a bad idea.

### Michalis and Alexis

The taverna is run by Michalis and Alexis, two friends each with their own personality. Michalis 
has a dry sense of humor, a touch sarcastic — exactly my kind of humor. Alexis is a bit calmer, but 
just as friendly. They recognize you instantly when you come back, and always make time for a chat.

Michalis's wife runs the kitchen. She's behind the food that makes Me Raki so special: honest, 
without frills, and always with a homey touch.

### Why We Keep Coming Back

Since that first time, we've come here regularly. Sometimes for a quick lunch on the road, 
sometimes for an evening full of small plates, wine and conversation. The food is always good, but 
it's mainly the atmosphere that keeps bringing us back. No rush, no crowds, just good food in good 
company.

It's the kind of place where you feel at ease right away, even if it's your first time there.

### In Closing

Me Raki Taverna isn't a big or flashy restaurant. You could easily drive right past it. But once 
you know it, you won't forget it. The name really says it all: here you eat with raki, but above 
all with meraki — with love, passion and feeling. And you can taste that in everything they do.

### Tip

Skip the standard [moussaka](/recepten/moussaka) and go for something truly local: skioufichta 
(homemade Cretan pasta), snails, fried liver, or fresh sardines and grilled squid. Those are the 
dishes the chef is visibly proud of.

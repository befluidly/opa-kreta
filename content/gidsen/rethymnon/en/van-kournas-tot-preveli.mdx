\*You can technically do this route from many places on Crete – as long as you leave early enough. 
From Rethymno, it's the most straightforward, but those staying in Heraklion, Chania or even 
(Limenas) Chersonissos can drive the same loop too. Just plan for some extra driving time early in 
the morning and on the way back.\*

\*On this day trip, we set off from Rethymno toward Lake Kournas, via the historic 
Lappa/Argyroupoli to the Kourtaliotiko Gorge, ending at Preveli Monastery and – if there's still 
time and energy – Preveli Beach. From there, you head back to Rethymno.\*

## Rethymno

Rethymno sits centrally on Crete's north coast and combines an atmospheric old town with good 
access to the interior. The city grew into a regional trading center during the Venetian period, 
and you can still see plenty of traces of that: wooden balconies, narrow alleys, the elegant 
Rimondi Fountain and, of course, the Fortezza, the 16th-century fortress atop the hill.

Because nearly all the sights sit close together, Rethymno feels compact and lively. Taverns, 
cafés and small shops are scattered throughout the city, and from the center you can drive to the 
first stop of this day trip in under ten minutes.

## Lake Kournas

Lake Kournas is Crete's only natural freshwater lake. It sits in a bowl-shaped valley, formed by 
limestone and ancient karst processes. Because of the composition of the ground, the water is 
remarkably clear, making it a favorable habitat for turtles and various bird species. The area is 
therefore included in the Natura 2000 program.

Historically, Kournas was already mentioned in Venetian documents as an important water source. 
Centuries ago the lake was larger; even today, the water level still rises considerably in winter.

Along the shore are a few taverns and pedal boat rental stands. Many visitors take a pedal boat 
out onto the lake, but even a short walk along the water's edge already gives a good sense of the 
surroundings. The village of Kournas itself used to be known for its potters — something you can 
still occasionally spot in local shops.

A stay of about an hour is enough to experience the lake while still leaving plenty of time for 
the rest of the day.

## Lappa & Argyroupoli

The route continues further inland, toward Argyroupoli, a village built on top of the remains of 
the ancient Lappa. According to tradition, even Agamemnon, king of Mycenae, is said to have 
founded the city. What's certain is that Lappa was a regional power especially during the 
Hellenistic and Roman periods, even minting its own coins. After a severe earthquake and later 
invasions, the city fell into decline, after which the current Argyroupoli developed among and on 
top of the ruins.

Walking through the village, you'll notice that many buildings incorporate Roman foundations or 
pieces of ancient stone into their walls. Just outside the center are springs and small 
waterfalls that have been used for centuries — first by the Romans, then by the Byzantines and 
Venetians.

### Lappa Avocado – The Avocado Shop

Thanks to the mild microclimate, a lot of avocados are grown in this region. That gave rise to 
Lappa Avocado, a small local brand producing avocado oil and skincare products. Their shop in the 
village makes for a nice quick stop: small bottles of oil, soaps, creams and other natural 
products, all sourced from the immediate area.

### Argyroupoli – Springs and Waterfalls

The waterfalls lie in a green valley just outside the village. It's a compact area with flowing 
water, fig trees, grapevines and taverns built above the water streams. It's a lovely spot for 
lunch — fresh vegetables, local cheeses and simple mountain dishes are served here with a view of 
the water.

Plan for 1 to 1.5 hours for the village, the springs and possibly lunch.

## Kourtaliotiko Gorge

After Argyroupoli, you continue south, where the road cuts through the impressive Kourtaliotiko 
Gorge. The name comes from the word kourtala: the clapping sound created when the wind blows 
through cracks in the rocks.

The gorge was carved out by the Megalos Potamos river and today is an important habitat for birds 
of prey such as vultures. Halfway through the gorge stands the chapel of Osios Nikolaos 
Kourtaliotis, dedicated to a 17th-century monk who lived in this region.

For this day trip, Kourtaliotiko is a short, focused stop:

- park at the viewpoint
- descend a short way via the stairs toward the chapel
- along the way you'll see and hear the small waterfalls from above

The descent is relatively short but steep. Good shoes come in handy here. You don't need to go all 
the way down to the riverbed or the furthest part of the gorge to still get a good sense of the 
surroundings.

\*Important: Those who want to walk the entire gorge and spend more time by the river certainly 
can – but then Kourtaliotiko immediately becomes the last major stop of the day. In that case, 
it's more realistic to drive back toward Rethymno afterward and skip Preveli, or plan Preveli for 
another day.\*

For most travelers, 30–45 minutes is enough to experience the gorge, visit the chapel and take 
photos.

## Preveli Monastery

You continue on to the south coast and reach Preveli Monastery, one of Crete's most historic 
monasteries. Although its origins likely date back to the 10th century, most of the current 
buildings date from the 16th and 17th centuries.

Over the centuries, Preveli was known as a stronghold of local resistance. During various Cretan 
uprisings against the Ottomans, the monks offered shelter and support. That same pattern repeated 
during the German occupation, when Allied soldiers were helped to escape by boat via the 
monastery. For that reason, Preveli was later looted, but it was subsequently rebuilt.

The monastery is relatively small but well-maintained. Within its walls you'll find:

- the main church, with icons and frescoes
- a small museum room with religious objects and photographs, including some from the World War 
II period, when the monastery helped Allied soldiers
- a courtyard where you can walk around quietly for a while

The combination of monastic life, history and the view means most visitors easily spend 45–60 
minutes here.

Also keep in mind here that you should be dressed appropriately: no very short shorts, no bare 
shoulders in the church or in areas clearly used for religious purposes.

## Optional – Preveli Palm Beach

Those with time and energy to spare can drive on from the monastery to the parking area for 
Preveli Beach. From there, you descend a staircase toward the mouth of the river.

The valley is known for its palm forest of the Phoenix theophrastii species, the same type found 
at Vai. In the sixties and seventies, Preveli became popular with backpackers, but the area itself 
is older and was previously used mainly by shepherds.

The beach itself lies at the river mouth, surrounded by palms. It makes for a beautiful way to end 
the day, but keep in mind:

- the descent and climb back up: these are fairly demanding when it's hot
- the time: count on an extra 1.5 to 2 hours if you also want to swim or walk along part of the 
river

If your day is already full, or you'd rather take it easy, you can also skip Preveli Beach and 
save it for a separate trip. In that case, the monastery itself feels like a fitting final "real" 
stop.

## Back to Rethymno

After Preveli, you drive back to Rethymno via the south coast and the mountains. The route itself 
is worthwhile: alternating views of the sea, villages and mountain slopes.

Those coming from Heraklion, Chania or Chersonissos simply follow the same road back north and 
pick up the highway toward their own base again. Depending on where you're staying, you'll be back 
in the early evening or later that night.

## Practical Information

- **Total distance**: approximately 115 km (round trip)
- **Driving time**: 3 to 3.5 hours excluding stops
- **From Chersonissos/Heraklion/Chania**: add 1 to 1.5 hours, depending on traffic and exact 
location
- **Recommended time**: full day trip (depart in the morning, return in the early evening)
- **What to bring**: Sturdy sandals or sneakers for the stairs at Kourtaliotiko and possibly 
Preveli Beach, respectful clothing for the monastery: long pants or a long skirt and a t-shirt 
covering the shoulders, water and sun protection, especially in summer!

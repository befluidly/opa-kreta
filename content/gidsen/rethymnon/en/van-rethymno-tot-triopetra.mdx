## Rethymno

Rethymno is Crete's third city and lies roughly halfway between Chania and 
[Heraklion](/gidsen/heraklion/bezoek-heraklion). It's a compact, lively city that has kept its 
Venetian character well. The old town is a maze of narrow alleys, mansions with wooden balconies, 
shops, cafés and small hotels.

The main attractions all sit close together. The **Fortezza**, the large fortress above the city, 
was built in the sixteenth century by the Venetians. From its walls you get an excellent view over 
Rethymno and the sea. In the lower town you'll find the **Venetian Harbor**, the **Rimondi 
Fountain** and several Ottoman mosques that recall the Turkish period.

Of all the major cities on Crete, [Rethymno](/gidsen/rethymnon/bezoek-rethymno) is, after 
Heraklion (my home base), the most beautiful. In my view, it offers more than Chania: an 
atmospheric harbor, small streets that feel like walking through a village, cozy taverns and a 
stunning view over the sea from the Fortezza.

Rethymno is also a great starting point for a day trip south. From the center, you can 
[drive](/praktisch/auto-huren-op-kreta) out of the city in under ten minutes, heading toward the 
Arkadi Monastery.

## Moni Arkadi

The **Arkadi Monastery** lies about 25 kilometers from Rethymno. It's one of Crete's best-known 
monasteries, not only for its architecture but especially for its role in Cretan history. During 
the uprising against the Ottomans in 1866, hundreds of Cretans, including women and children, took 
refuge here. When the siege could no longer be held, they detonated the gunpowder room — with 
themselves inside — rather than be taken prisoner.

That dramatic moment turned Arkadi into a symbol of freedom and resistance. Greece's national 
motto, _Eleftheria i thanatos_ ("Freedom or Death"), takes on a tangible meaning here.

Today, Arkadi is a peaceful monastery complex, well-maintained and open to visitors. The museum 
holds objects and documents from the uprising. Outside, you can walk among the gardens and the 
courtyard with its Renaissance-style double church.

## Patsos Gorge

From Arkadi, you continue south toward Meronas and Patsos. The **Patsos** gorge lies in a green 
valley, about 25 minutes' drive away. The entrance is next to a small taverna where you can park.

The trail follows a stream and runs through a narrow [gorge](/gidsen/natuur-en-wandelen/de-mooiste-kloven). 
The route isn't long or strenuous, but the surroundings are impressive: rock walls, ferns and the 
sound of flowing water. Halfway along, you reach the cave church of **Agios Antonios**, literally 
built into the rock face. Candles are often still burning inside — a quiet, special place.

After the walk, you can grab a drink or a bite at the taverna. It's simple, but typically Cretan.

## Spili

**Spili** lies a bit further along, roughly halfway to the south coast. The village is known for 
its fountain with lion-head spouts, from which cool mountain water flows. Along the main street 
you'll find taverns, herb shops and small workshops with olive-wood objects.

It's a good time for a lunch break. Most taverns serve local dishes: fried eggplant, goat cheese, 
salads and mountain herb tea. The village sits a bit higher up and offers a pleasant break from 
the heat on warm days.

## Triopetra

After Spili, the road continues through an open, mountainous landscape. After about three-quarters 
of an hour's drive, you arrive at **Triopetra**, a large sandy beach on the south coast. It's one 
of Crete's quietest and most pleasant beaches. There's plenty of space, even in summer, and it 
always stays relaxed.

Triopetra consists of two parts: one with sunbeds and umbrellas, and an open stretch of beach 
where you can simply lay your towel, like on the Belgian coast. The photo on Opa's Kreta's 
homepage is actually of Triopetra — it captures well just how spacious and peaceful it is.

The taverna on the left, **Taverna Apothiki**, is my go-to stop. Ideal for a drink or a lunch, 
with a sea view. It's a relaxed place to sit, without crowds or expectations. No one bats an eye 
if you stay for a few hours without ordering more.

## Practical Information

- **Total distance**: approximately 120 km (round trip)
- **Driving time**: 3 to 3.5 hours excluding stops
- **Recommended time**: full day trip (depart in the morning, return in the early evening)
